The moss does not hurry.  
It keeps the rain a little longer  
than the stone would like,  
and calls that a home.

I came here with a question  
folded small in my pocket,  
the kind you carry for weeks  
until the edges soften.

The birches answered nothing.  
The creek said the same word  
over and over, gently,  
the way you calm a child.

And somewhere between the ferns  
and the grey light leaning in,  
I stopped asking  
and started listening.

Maybe that is all a making is —  
borrowing the patience of green things,  
returning it slightly changed,  
almost original.
